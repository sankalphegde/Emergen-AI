**Results**
We evaluated five approaches for prioritizing critical patients at ED intake: a rule-based triage baseline, a context-aware logistic model, a matrix-factorization model, a graph-based model with patient-attribute embeddings, and a pairwise learning-to-rank model. To prioritize critical patients, we used a validation-tuned threshold that maximized F2 score (favoring recall), with the label defined as acuity ≤ 2.

Across models, top‑k relevance is strong and fairly consistent (Precision@5 ≈ 0.951–0.966, Recall@5 ≈ 0.825–0.839; Precision@10 ≈ 0.951–0.954, Recall@10 ≈ 0.964–0.967). NDCG was added and shows similar ordering quality (`NDCG@5`: baseline 0.962, context 0.984, matrix factorization 0.968). Differentiation appears in global discrimination metrics. The context-aware model remains strongest (`ROC-AUC = 0.796`, `PR-AUC = 0.793`, `F1 = 0.715`, `F2 = 0.849`, `accuracy = 0.603`). Matrix factorization is competitive on F2 (`0.841`) but weaker on AUC (`ROC-AUC = 0.597`). The graph and pairwise rankers did not learn meaningful separation (`F1/F2 = 0.0`, `ROC-AUC = 0.50`).

Overall, the context-aware model provided the best balance for safety-critical prioritization while remaining interpretable and deployable.

**Discussion**
The strong Precision@k, Recall@k, and NDCG@k across models suggest that the most critical cases are consistently ranked near the top, likely driven by vitals and acuity indicators. However, thresholded classification exposes meaningful differences: the context-aware model is substantially better at identifying borderline critical cases, which is reflected in higher F1/F2 and AUCs.

The graph-based model underperformed relative to the context model, likely because the available graph attributes (chief complaint, transport, race) were too coarse to add meaningful structure. The pairwise ranker also did not learn effectively, likely due to limited positive-negative pairs within hourly groups. These results support the project’s hypothesis that triage is better framed as a ranking and prioritization problem, but they also highlight the need for richer operational signals (bed availability, staffing, real queue length) to fully realize graph-based or pairwise advantages.
